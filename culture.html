The AO Culture 5

Culture is not simply what is written in the handbook. Culture is how we behave when the office is busy, when something goes wrong, when nobody is watching, when a teammate needs help, and when a patient is sitting in our chair.

Five pillars

These five incorporate the original Alexandria Orthodontics handbook values while more clearly defining the behaviors we expect from one another every day. Our culture should guide the small decisions we make throughout every day.

1

OWN IT

Take responsibility. Be reliable. Follow through.

We take ownership of our responsibilities, our decisions, our communication, our mistakes, and our results. Accountability is not simply admitting that something went wrong. It means understanding your role in the outcome and taking responsibility for what happens next. We do not waste energy deciding whose fault something is. We ask: what is my responsibility here, and what can I do next?

Accountability

We acknowledge and assume responsibility for our actions, decisions, responsibilities, and communication. When we make a mistake, we acknowledge it. When something is our responsibility, we do not wait to be reminded repeatedly. When we agree to do something, we follow through.

No excuses

There may be legitimate reasons why something happened. There is still a responsibility to solve the problem. Explaining a situation is different from using it as an excuse.

  • Nobody told me.
  • That's not my job.
  • I thought someone else was doing it.
  • They didn't do their part.
  • I didn't know.
  • I was busy.

Instead: here is what happened, here is what I could have done differently, and here is what I am going to do now.

No shifting blame

Mistakes should lead to learning, not finger-pointing. If three people were involved in a breakdown, all three should examine their own role. Accountability begins with yourself.

Integrity

We tell the truth even when the truth is uncomfortable. We document accurately. We do not hide mistakes. We do not change the story to make ourselves look better. We communicate problems early enough for someone to help solve them.

Integrity means doing the right thing even when nobody is watching.

Reliability

If the team cannot depend on you, the team cannot function effectively.

  • Arriving on time or early
  • Being prepared
  • Completing assigned responsibilities
  • Following through without repeated reminders
  • Returning to unfinished tasks
  • Communicating when something cannot be completed
  • Being where you are supposed to be
  • Doing what you said you would do

Consistency

Once the team agrees on a process, we follow it until it is officially changed. We do not create a different version of the procedure every time we perform it.

  • Predictability
  • Quality
  • Safety
  • Efficiency
  • Easier training
  • Better communication

Focus

Being present at work means being mentally present as well. We minimize unnecessary distractions and give our attention to the task in front of us. When working with a patient, the patient should feel that they have our attention. When receiving instructions, we listen. When performing a clinical procedure, we focus. When documenting, we document accurately.

What OWN IT looks like

  • A team member realizes she forgot something: "I missed that. I'll take care of it now, and I'll make sure it is part of my checklist next time."
  • A team member realizes he will not finish an assigned task and tells the appropriate person before it becomes an emergency.
  • A technician notices she documented something incorrectly and corrects it rather than hoping nobody notices.
  • Someone receives constructive feedback and changes the behavior instead of explaining why the feedback should not apply.

What OWN IT does not look like

  • Blaming the schedule
  • Blaming another department
  • Blaming another team member
  • Hiding mistakes
  • Waiting to be reminded repeatedly
  • Being chronically late
  • Leaving unfinished tasks for someone else without communication
  • Saying "I didn't know" without attempting to learn
  • Ignoring a known problem because nobody specifically assigned it to you
Simple AO reminder See your responsibility. Accept your responsibility. Finish your responsibility.
2

FIND A WAY

Take initiative. Solve problems. Be resourceful.

We do not simply identify problems. We actively participate in solving them. We want an office filled with people who can think. Autonomy does not mean doing whatever you want. It means developing enough knowledge, judgment, and confidence to handle appropriate problems without immediately transferring every decision to somebody else.

Three questions to ask first

Before saying "I don't know," ask: how can I find out?

Before saying "I can't do this," ask: what can I try?

Before handing a problem to someone else, ask: what have I already done to solve it?

Initiative

Do not wait to be told every obvious next step. If you see something that needs to be done and you are capable of doing it, take appropriate action. Initiative means looking one step ahead.

  • What needs to happen next?
  • What will the doctor need?
  • What will my teammate need?
  • What will the patient need?
  • What can I prepare now?
  • What problem is likely to occur next, and can I prevent it?

Autonomy

We want team members who learn how to make appropriate decisions. Early in training you will need help. As you gain experience, the goal is to require less supervision for routine situations. Growth should produce greater independence.

Resourcefulness

Before escalating a routine question, consider the resources available to you.

  • Have I checked the training materials?
  • Have I reviewed the procedure?
  • Have I checked the patient's chart?
  • Have I looked at the treatment card?
  • Have I checked the schedule?
  • Have I reviewed our protocol?
  • Have I asked the appropriate team member?
  • Have I attempted to reason through the problem?

Resourcefulness does not mean guessing. It means making a reasonable effort to find the correct answer.

Hunger

We want people who are hungry to improve.

  • Learn something new
  • Take on greater responsibility
  • Develop new skills
  • Become faster
  • Become more accurate
  • Help another department
  • Understand the bigger picture

Avoiding complacency

Being capable today does not guarantee that you will remain excellent tomorrow. We should never reach the point where we believe we are good enough and no longer need to improve. There is always another level.

An important distinction

Resourcefulness does not mean never asking for help. It means trying to understand and solve appropriate problems before unnecessarily transferring them to someone else.

Immediate escalation is absolutely appropriate in situations involving:

  • Patient safety
  • Clinical concerns outside your responsibility
  • Emergencies
  • Significant patient complaints
  • Uncertainty that could cause harm
  • Financial or administrative decisions outside your authority

Knowing when to ask for help is itself a skill.

What FIND A WAY looks like

  • A tech notices a problem, checks the chart, checks the protocol, tries the appropriate solution, then approaches the coordinator: "Here's the problem. Here's what I checked. Here's what I think we should do."
  • Someone looks one step ahead and prepares what the doctor, the teammate, or the patient will need next.
  • A question gets answered by checking the training resource instead of interrupting someone else.
  • A team member escalates immediately when the issue involves patient safety.

What FIND A WAY does not look like

  • "I don't know what to do," with nothing attempted first
  • Guessing instead of checking the correct answer
  • Transferring every routine decision to someone else
  • Waiting to be told each obvious next step
  • Believing you are good enough and no longer need to improve
  • Treating established systems as optional while calling it initiative
Simple AO reminder Think first. Use your resources. Bring solutions, not just problems.
3

ONE TEAM

Communicate. Support each other. Respect each other.

We help each other no matter what. Alexandria Orthodontics does not function as a collection of individuals working independently. We function as one team working toward one goal. The success of the clinic matters more than whether something technically belongs to my area.

Teamwork

The question is not simply whether I am caught up. The better question is whether we are caught up. When your responsibilities are under control, look around. Someone else may need help.

Support

If someone is struggling, we do not stand by and watch. Sometimes support means physically assisting. Sometimes it means answering a question. Sometimes it means taking over a responsibility. Sometimes it means simply recognizing that someone is overwhelmed and asking what you can do.

Communication

Good teamwork depends on communication. That means communicating early, clearly, directly, respectfully, and to the correct person.

Communication should prevent surprises. A problem communicated early is often manageable. The same problem communicated ten minutes later may become an emergency.

Delegation

Delegation is not dumping work on someone else. Good delegation involves:

  • Clearly identifying what needs to happen
  • Identifying who is responsible
  • Confirming that the person understands
  • Communicating important context
  • Following up when appropriate

Closed-loop communication

When something important is communicated, there should be acknowledgment.

"Chair 3 has two broken brackets and will need Dr. Lee in approximately three minutes."

"Understood. Chair 3 first. I'll transition Dr. Lee when you're ready."

That closes the communication loop.

Respect

We communicate professionally even when we are busy, frustrated, behind, stressed, tired, or disagreeing. Stress does not give us permission to disrespect one another.

  • Tone of voice
  • Word choice
  • Listening
  • Body language
  • Allowing someone to finish speaking
  • Giving feedback appropriately
  • Receiving feedback professionally

Diversity

Different backgrounds, experiences, personalities, perspectives, and strengths make the team stronger. We treat people fairly and respectfully. People should feel that they belong on the team and that their contributions matter.

Psychological safety

Team members should be comfortable saying any of these out loud:

  • I made a mistake.
  • I need help.
  • I don't understand this.
  • I think something may be unsafe.
  • I have an idea.
  • I see a potential problem.

People should not hide concerns because they are afraid of being embarrassed.

Physical and clinical safety

Safety is everyone's responsibility. If you notice something unsafe, speak up. Safety is not exclusively the responsibility of the person performing the procedure.

  • Infection control
  • Patient positioning
  • Instrument handling
  • Sharps
  • Equipment
  • Clinical procedures
  • Workplace hazards
  • Communication breakdowns

What ONE TEAM looks like

  • Your area is caught up, so you help the person next to you.
  • A concern gets raised early, while it is still manageable.
  • An important instruction is acknowledged back, so the loop is closed.
  • Someone speaks up about a safety concern in another person's procedure.
  • A new team member feels able to say "I don't understand this" without embarrassment.

What ONE TEAM does not look like

  • "That's not my job."
  • Watching someone become overwhelmed
  • Withholding information
  • Gossiping
  • Talking disrespectfully about teammates
  • Passive-aggressive communication
  • Failing to communicate because "they should have known"
  • Refusing to help because your own work is complete
  • Creating unnecessary conflict between departments
  • Blaming instead of solving
Simple AO reminder Communicate early. Help quickly. Treat people with respect.
4

BETTER EVERY DAY

Learn. Improve. Train. Build better systems.

We should be better tomorrow because of what we learned today. We do not expect perfection. We expect progress. Compare yourself to who you were yesterday, not to who someone else is today. Every mistake, challenge, delay, complaint, training opportunity, and difficult patient interaction can teach us something.

Continuous improvement

We continually develop:

  • Clinical skills
  • Communication skills
  • Efficiency
  • Leadership skills
  • Technical knowledge
  • Patient-service skills
  • Systems
  • Processes

Learning

Training does not end when orientation ends. Everyone should continue learning. The strongest team members are often the people who keep asking how they can do this better.

Receiving feedback

Feedback is not punishment. Feedback is information. When someone identifies an opportunity for improvement, our first reaction should be curiosity rather than defensiveness.

  • What am I missing?
  • What could I do differently?
  • Can you show me?
  • What should this look like next time?

Training others

Learning becomes even more valuable when it can be transferred. Experienced team members have a responsibility to help develop newer team members, and training others strengthens your own understanding. If the organization depends on only one person knowing how to do something, we have a vulnerability.

Systems thinking

Great offices do not rely entirely on memory or heroics. They create reliable systems. Whenever a recurring problem occurs, we ask whether this is a people problem or a system problem. Sometimes someone needs additional training. Other times the process itself needs to be improved.

  • Checklists
  • Written procedures
  • Training videos
  • Standardized notes
  • Defined handoffs
  • Clear responsibilities
  • Visual systems
  • Templates
  • Better technology
  • Better communication

Consistency

Once we develop a good system, everyone should follow it. We should not have five different versions of the same procedure unless there is a legitimate reason.

  • Train faster
  • Identify problems
  • Improve quality
  • Reduce mistakes
  • Improve safety
  • Increase efficiency

Innovation

Just because we have always done something one way does not mean it is the best way. Ideas are welcome, in technology, patient communication, scheduling, clinical technique, training, workflow, marketing, documentation, and patient education.

Innovation does not mean ignoring established systems. The correct sequence is: understand the system, identify an opportunity, propose an improvement, test it, then adopt it as a team.

Balance

Continuous improvement should be sustainable. We believe in maintaining a healthy work-life balance. Hustle does not mean burning people out. At AO, hustle means that when we are working, we work with purpose, urgency, focus, and energy.

Sustainable excellence means performing at a high level while respecting the health and well-being of the team.

Never become complacent

Success can become dangerous when it causes us to stop improving. We should be proud of what we have built without believing that we are finished building it.

What BETTER EVERY DAY looks like

  • "This problem keeps happening, so I think we should change the process."
  • Feedback is met with curiosity: what am I missing, and what should this look like next time?
  • An experienced team member trains someone else rather than staying the only person who knows.
  • A recurring problem is examined as a system problem, not just a people problem.
  • An improvement is proposed, tested, and then adopted by the whole team.

What BETTER EVERY DAY does not look like

  • Becoming defensive whenever feedback is given
  • Creating personal versions of standardized systems
  • Ignoring established procedures in the name of a new idea
  • Relying on memory and heroics instead of building a system
  • Hoarding knowledge instead of training others
  • Believing we are finished building
  • Treating hustle as a reason to burn people out
Simple AO reminder Learn it. Practice it. Teach it. Improve it.

PATIENT FIRST

Deliver exceptional care and create raving fans.

Every system, every role, and every team member ultimately exists to serve the patient. Excellent orthodontics alone is not enough. Our patients should receive both excellent clinical care and an exceptional experience. Our goal is to create raving fans.

Quality of care

We provide attentive, thoughtful, high-quality orthodontic care. That means paying attention to details rather than simply completing an appointment.

Attentiveness

Patients should feel seen.

  • Greet them
  • Use their name
  • Listen
  • Acknowledge parents and family members
  • Explain what we are doing
  • Recognize concerns
  • Respond to discomfort
  • Avoid making patients feel rushed through an assembly line

Positive experience

Positivity is part of patient care. We want the office to feel friendly, helpful, organized, energetic, professional, and welcoming.

Positivity does not mean pretending problems do not exist. It means approaching problems with an attitude oriented toward solutions.

Transparent communication

Patients deserve honest, understandable communication.

  • What we are doing
  • Why we are doing it
  • What we need from the patient
  • What happened if something went wrong
  • What the next step will be

Never allow confusion to continue simply because a difficult conversation would be uncomfortable.

Documentation

If it is important, document it. The next team member should not have to reconstruct what happened from memory.

  • Continuity of care
  • Communication
  • Accountability
  • Safety
  • Treatment planning
  • Follow-up

Safety

Patient safety comes before speed. We never sacrifice safety simply because the schedule is behind.

  • Infection control
  • Proper PPE
  • Correct procedures
  • Accurate communication
  • Correct patient identification
  • Clinical judgment
  • Equipment safety
  • Documentation

Gratitude

Our patients allow Alexandria Orthodontics to exist. Every patient who walks through the door is choosing to trust us with their health, their smile, their time, their money, and their family. We should never allow familiarity to make us forget that privilege.

Community

Our responsibility extends beyond individual appointments. Alexandria Orthodontics should make a positive contribution to the community we serve. Our reputation is built through thousands of interactions over many years, and every interaction contributes to what Alexandria Orthodontics represents.

The Patient First test

When making a decision, ask: what creates the best combination of quality, safety, communication, and experience for the patient? The answer will often make the correct decision clearer.

Patient-centered care does not mean saying yes to every patient request. It does not mean ignoring systems, allowing disrespect toward the team, or compromising treatment quality. Sometimes putting the patient first means having a difficult conversation, enforcing a policy, slowing down, correcting a mistake, or saying no. The objective is always the patient's long-term best interest.

What PATIENT FIRST looks like

  • "The patient looked confused, so I stopped and explained what we were doing."
  • A patient is greeted by name and their parent is acknowledged.
  • Something goes wrong and the patient is told honestly what happened and what comes next.
  • The schedule is behind and safety still comes before speed.
  • A difficult conversation happens because it serves the patient's long-term interest.

What PATIENT FIRST does not look like

  • Sacrificing patient experience because the office is busy
  • Sacrificing quality because the office is behind
  • Rushing patients through like an assembly line
  • Letting confusion continue to avoid an uncomfortable conversation
  • Poor documentation that leaves the next team member guessing
  • Saying yes to every request regardless of the patient's long-term interest
Simple AO reminder Quality care. Clear communication. Exceptional experience.

The three non-negotiables

Three principles that should exist inside every AO cultural value. They do not need to become additional pillars because they should be present everywhere.

Respect

We treat patients, parents, coworkers, doctors, vendors, and visitors professionally. Respect does not disappear when we are stressed.

Integrity

We tell the truth. We document honestly. We admit mistakes. We do the right thing when nobody is watching.

Safety

We protect our patients and our team. If something appears unsafe, we speak up. Safety always takes priority over convenience.

Culture is behavior, not personality

We are not asking everyone to have the same personality. Someone can be quiet, outgoing, serious, funny, introverted, extroverted, new, or experienced and still represent AO culture extremely well.

A quiet person can communicate exceptionally well. An introverted person can provide extraordinary patient care. A new employee can demonstrate tremendous accountability. A naturally independent person can become an exceptional teammate.

Culture is not about everyone acting the same. Culture is about everyone operating from the same fundamental expectations.

Culture is a two-way commitment

Culture cannot be something leadership asks employees to practice while exempting leadership from the same expectations. Doctors, managers, coordinators, trainers, and team members all operate within the same framework.

We ask team members to

  • Be accountable
  • Improve
  • Communicate
  • Support others
  • Follow systems
  • Take initiative

Leadership must also

  • Provide adequate training
  • Create clear expectations
  • Build understandable systems
  • Listen to feedback
  • Give people the tools necessary to succeed
  • Recognize good performance
  • Correct problems consistently
  • Provide opportunities to grow
  • Treat people respectfully
  • Protect employee safety
  • Support reasonable work-life balance

What we no longer normalize

Culture becomes clearer when we identify not only what we want, but also what we will no longer accept as normal. These are not merely undesirable behaviors. They conflict with the culture we are intentionally trying to build.

  • "That's not my job."
  • "Nobody told me."
  • "I don't know" without attempting to find out
  • Watching another team member struggle
  • Chronic lateness
  • Repeated reminders for routine responsibilities
  • Blaming coworkers
  • Hiding mistakes
  • Gossip
  • Disrespectful communication
  • Ignoring established procedures
  • Creating personal versions of standardized systems
  • Waiting for someone else to solve every problem
  • Complaining without participating in a solution
  • Becoming defensive whenever feedback is given
  • Ignoring safety concerns
  • Poor documentation
  • Sacrificing patient experience because the office is busy
  • Sacrificing quality because the office is behind
  • Complacency

The standard

The goal is not perfection. People will make mistakes. People will occasionally become overwhelmed. People will need help. People will misunderstand something. People will have difficult days.

The cultural expectation is not "never make a mistake." The expectation is:

Own the mistake. Help solve the problem. Communicate with the team. Learn from what happened. Protect the patient's experience.

Our culture

At Alexandria Orthodontics, we believe great patient care begins with a great team.

We OWN IT by taking responsibility for our actions, decisions, communication, and results.

We FIND A WAY by taking initiative, thinking critically, using our resources, and participating in solutions.

We operate as ONE TEAM by communicating clearly, supporting one another, and treating each other with respect.

We become BETTER EVERY DAY by learning from our experiences, developing our skills, training one another, improving our systems, and refusing to become complacent.

And through everything we do, we put the PATIENT FIRST by providing exceptional care, clear communication, a safe environment, and an experience worthy of their trust.

We act with integrity. We protect the safety of our patients and our team. We treat people with respect. We are grateful for the opportunity to serve our patients, support each other, and contribute to our community.

Own it. Find a way. One team. Better every day. Patient first.
That is Alexandria Orthodontics.
