Team meetings

Operational working meetings held every two weeks, one for Clinical and one for Front Desk. Prepare. Discuss. Solve. Assign. Follow up.

Purpose

The morning huddle handles today. The team meeting handles the pattern behind today. If the same problem shows up in three huddles, it belongs here.

  • Anticipate staffing and schedule issues before they become daily problems.
  • Communicate important team and office updates.
  • Identify recurring operational problems and build solutions.
  • Introduce, refine, and reinforce systems.
  • Address training needs and role clarity.
  • Document action points and assign ownership.
  • Create accountability through follow-up at the next meeting.
What do we need to know? What problem are we solving? What are we going to do? Who owns the follow-through? The core meeting standard.

The two meetings

Both run 60 minutes every two weeks. You attend the one for your department.

MeetingAttendeesLeadLength
Clinical Team Meeting Clinical staff, Lab team, Clinical Team Leaders Clinic Coordinator and Clinical Team Lead 60 min
Front Desk Team Meeting Front Desk, Office Manager, Treatment Coordinators Office Manager 60 min

Preparation

The meeting is not the time to decide what to discuss. Leaders prepare the agenda and time limits before the meeting starts.

Leader preparation checklist

  • Review unresolved action points from the previous meeting.
  • Review the calendar and important upcoming dates.
  • Identify doctors or team members who will be in or out.
  • Review vacations and coverage needs.
  • Review doctor schedule and any schedule-template changes.
  • Gather team updates: new hires, departures, promotions, training, role changes, and payroll, PTO, or system changes.
  • Collect department problems that require discussion or a decision.
  • Identify housekeeping reminders that need reinforcement.
  • Decide which topics require a decision, which require training, and which are only informational.
  • Prepare the agenda and time limits before the meeting starts.

Team member preparation

  • Submit important issues before the meeting when possible.
  • Come prepared to explain the problem clearly.
  • Bring possible solutions, not only complaints.
  • Know the facts needed for the discussion.
  • Bring a notebook or device for action points.

Ask yourself before the meeting: what issue does our team need to discuss, solve, clarify, or plan for?

The standard 60-minute agenda

A repeatable structure for both departments. Same six sections, same order, every time.

SectionTimePurpose
1. Calendar5 min Upcoming dates, closures, team events, people in and out, deadlines
2. Team updates8 min People, roles, training, payroll and PTO systems, new processes
3. Schedule10 min Coverage, doctor schedule, templates, vacations, capacity changes
4. Department discussion22 min Identify problems, find solutions, build systems
5. Housekeeping5 min Short reminders and standards
6. Action points10 min Assign owners, due dates, documentation, follow-up
Total60 min Finish with clear decisions and owners
If one topic needs a much longer discussion, assign a smaller working group and continue outside the 60-minute meeting. The time management rule.

Clinical Team Meeting

Clinical staff and Lab team. Led by the Clinic Coordinator and Clinical Team Lead, who prepare the agenda and unresolved action points ahead of time.

1. Calendar

  • Office closures and special hours
  • Team events and quarterly meetings
  • Doctors and clinical or lab team members in or out
  • Vacations, training days, important deadlines

2. Team updates

  • New or departing team members
  • Promotions and role changes
  • Training plans and competency needs
  • New systems affecting payroll, PTO, training, or team responsibilities

3. Schedule

  • Clinical coverage during vacations or absences
  • Doctor schedule and doctor-time capacity
  • Schedule-template or column changes
  • Appointment-time changes or seasonal schedules
  • Coverage changes created by part-time schedules or role changes

4. Clinical and lab discussion

The main working section. Identify what is not working, understand why, and build a repeatable solution.

  • Patient flow and chair coverage
  • Doctor-time efficiency
  • Bondings, debonds, retainer checks, scanning, and appliance workflows
  • Sterilization and tray workflow
  • Lab workflow, appliance status, inventory, and case handoff
  • Documentation and next-visit notes
  • Clinical training gaps or procedures requiring support
  • Equipment problems and room readiness
  • Communication between Clinical, Lab, Treatment Coordinators, and Front Desk
  • New clinical systems and how they will be taught and maintained

5. Housekeeping

  • Patient sunglasses
  • Bracket preparation and buttering in brackets
  • Restocking
  • Chair and tray cleanup
  • Complete notes and next-visit directions
  • Other short reminders that reinforce an existing standard

If the same housekeeping reminder keeps returning, it is probably a system or training problem. Move it into the discussion section.

Six problem-solving questions

Work any recurring problem through these in order. They turn a complaint into a system.

1What specifically is happening?

2Why is it happening?

3What should happen instead?

4What system will make the correct behavior repeatable?

5Who will build or document the system?

6How and when will we confirm it is working?

Front Desk Team Meeting

Front Desk, Office Manager, and Treatment Coordinators. Led by the Office Manager, who prepares the agenda and unresolved action points ahead of time.

1. Calendar

  • Office closures and special hours
  • Team events and quarterly meetings
  • Doctors and front desk or TC team members in or out
  • Vacations, interviews, training days, deadlines

2. Team updates

  • New or departing team members
  • Promotions or role changes
  • Training plans
  • New payroll, PTO, scheduling, communication, or administrative systems

3. Schedule

  • Front desk and TC coverage during absences
  • Doctor schedule and consultation availability
  • Template or appointment-type changes
  • Patient scheduling capacity and seasonal changes
  • Upcoming periods requiring special coverage

4. Operational discussion

Solve systems, communication, and workflow problems.

  • Phone workflow and patient priorities
  • Scheduling and appointment-type accuracy
  • Patient check-in and check-out
  • Consult workflow and treatment starts
  • Records, scans, and Treatment Coordinator handoff
  • Financial communication and recurring-payment workflow
  • Insurance and documentation systems
  • Patient forms, scripts, and follow-up responsibilities
  • Communication with Clinical and Lab
  • Staffing coverage and front desk role clarity
  • Training, new systems, and accountability

5. Housekeeping

  • Documentation reminders
  • Phone and scheduling scripts
  • Forms and consent workflow
  • Workspace standards
  • Check-in and check-out steps
  • Routine closing or follow-up tasks
Do not finish with "everyone knows now." Finish with "this is the new documented process, this person owns it, and this is when we will review it." When a discussion becomes a system.

Action points and follow-through

The meeting is not finished until ownership is clear.

Problem→ Solution→ Documented system→ Owner→ Due date→ Follow-up
StepQuestion it answers
ProblemWhat is happening?
SolutionWhat should happen instead?
SystemWhat process, checklist, script, or template needs to be documented?
OwnerWho is specifically responsible for completing or maintaining it?
Due dateWhen will it be completed or launched?
Follow-upWhen will leadership verify that it is working?

Team members who miss the meeting

  • Maintain brief meeting minutes with major decisions, schedule changes, new systems, and action points.
  • Team members who were absent must review the meeting summary when they return.
  • If a change directly affects how they perform their job, their Team Leader reviews it with them and confirms understanding.
  • Do not allow a system change to fail because one person was not present.
