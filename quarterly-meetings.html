Quarterly meeting

Four times a year the whole practice steps back from daily operations to strengthen Alexandria Orthodontics as a whole.

Format

Practice-wide training and development, held Q1, Q2, Q3, and Q4.

  • Typical duration is four to five hours.
  • The office is closed so the entire team can participate without patient-flow interruptions.
  • All Team Leaders prepare a focused presentation on a specific topic or connected set of topics.
  • The entire team is often involved through discussion, exercises, role-play, or problem-solving.

Themes that work well

  • Culture
  • Communication
  • Systems
  • Problem-solving
  • Accountability
  • Teamwork
  • Leadership
  • Efficiency
  • Patient experience
  • Practice standards

The AO Culture 5 is a natural fit for a culture-themed quarterly.

Leadership groups

Three groups present each quarter. Their sections should complement one another rather than repeat the same material.

GroupTypical focus
Doctors Practice vision, clinical philosophy, standards, culture, strategic systems
Front Desk Management Scheduling, patient communication, treatment coordination, financial and administrative systems
Clinical Leadership Clinical workflow, patient experience, communication, training, efficiency, standards

Leadership preparation

Plan weeks ahead, not the morning of the meeting.

  • Choose the quarterly theme and explain why it matters now.
  • Define the desired outcome: what should the team understand or do differently afterward?
  • Assign each leadership group a focused presentation or workshop.
  • Coordinate presentations so they complement one another instead of repeating the same material.
  • Prepare examples, videos, activities, worksheets, scenarios, or role-play as needed.
  • Decide how action points will be captured and who will record them.
  • Assign a timekeeper and facilitator.
  • Prepare the final action-point review and follow-up plan.

How to build a quarterly presentation

Five blocks. A presentation that stops after the first two will not change anything on Monday.

  • Block 1

    Teach

    Introduce the concept and why it matters.

  • Block 2

    Show

    Use examples, videos, demonstrations, or real AO scenarios.

  • Block 3

    Discuss

    Ask the team questions and identify current problems.

  • Block 4

    Practice

    Role-play, work through cases, or complete a group activity.

  • Block 5

    Apply

    Define what changes at AO and how it will be implemented.

Quarterly meeting standards

These build on the rules that apply to every meeting and matter most in a room this large.

•

Create a safe environment

Team members should be able to speak honestly and respectfully.

•

Stay on topic

Use the facilitator and timekeeper to move the meeting forward.

•

Participate

These are interactive meetings, not passive lectures. Participation is expected.

•

Be on time

That applies to the start of the day and to returning from every break.

•

Phones and smartwatches away

Unless they are required for the meeting.

•

Respect the person speaking

Avoid side conversations and whispering.

•

Come prepared to identify solutions

Not only problems.

Meeting roles

Facilitator

Keeps the meeting moving and manages discussion.

Timekeeper

Watches presentation and break times.

Action-point recorders

Capture decisions, owners, and follow-up dates.

Presenters

Teach and facilitate the assigned section.

Team Leaders

Support participation and help translate ideas into systems.

After the quarterly meeting

A good presentation is only useful if something changes afterward.

Close with practice-wide action points

  • What are we changing?
  • What does the new system look like?
  • Who owns the implementation?
  • When does it begin?
  • Who needs training?
  • Where will the system be documented?
  • When will leadership follow up?

Within the following week

  • Finalize and distribute meeting minutes.
  • Publish or update new systems, checklists, scripts, or training materials.
  • Assign required training.
  • Communicate with anyone who missed the meeting.
  • Review major action points with leadership.
  • Add relevant department-level issues to upcoming team meetings.
  • Use morning huddles to reinforce execution when the new system affects daily operations.

The follow-through cycle

MeetingRole in the system
Quarterly meetingTeach and establish the larger direction.
Team meetingBuild, refine, and maintain the department-level system.
