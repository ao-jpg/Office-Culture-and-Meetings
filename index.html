The three meetings

Start with the meeting you are being trained on. Each page stands on its own.

60 minutes2 departments

Q1, Q2, Q3, and Q4

The core meeting standard

Four questions that every meeting we hold has to answer before it ends.

What do we need to know?

Calendar, staffing, schedule, and anything that changed since the last meeting.

What problem are we solving?

Named specifically, with the facts needed to discuss it.

What are we going to do?

A decision, not a conversation that trails off.

Who owns the follow-through?

One name, one due date, one review.

The follow-through cycle

A discussion is not finished when everyone understands it. It is finished when it becomes a documented system with an owner.

Problem→ Solution→ Documented system→ Owner→ Due date→ Follow-up
  1. Identify the problem clearly.
  2. Agree on the solution.
  3. Create or update the documented system.
  4. Assign a specific owner.
  5. Set a due date or next review date.
  6. Follow up until the system is working and being maintained.

Which meeting does this belong in?

Most wasted meeting time comes from raising an issue in the wrong room. Use this to decide where a topic goes.

If the issue isBest forum
A problem affecting today's schedule or patient flow Morning huddle
A recurring department workflow, staffing, training, or system issue Team meeting
A practice-wide culture, communication, system, or development topic Quarterly meeting
A new system that needs reinforcement after launch Team meeting and morning huddle
A major initiative affecting multiple departments Quarterly meeting, then team meeting follow-through

Rules that apply to every meeting

These hold whether the meeting runs fifteen minutes or five hours. They are the reason our meetings stay short and stay useful.

1

Be on time

Arriving at the scheduled start time is late. Arrive early enough to be prepared and seated before the meeting begins.

The leader confirms that the team is present and identifies anyone late or absent. Repeated lateness is addressed outside of the meeting.

2

Leaders prepare the agenda beforehand

The meeting is not the time to decide what to discuss. Whoever leads reviews unresolved action points, gathers the topics, and sets time limits before anyone sits down.

3

Come prepared

A meeting is not the time to begin reviewing the material. Anyone responsible for a portion of the agenda should have already reviewed their area. Know the facts needed for the discussion.

4

Bring a notebook

Bring a notebook or device for action points. Record what was discussed, the action items for your department, who owns each one, and anything that has to be passed along. These notes become the meeting minutes.

5

Phones away

Phones and smartwatches are put away unless they are required for the meeting. Everyone should be listening and participating.

6

Eat beforehand, not during

Meals happen before the meeting. Meeting time requires full attention.

7

Everyone participates

A meeting is not a presentation given by one person while everyone else listens. Each department reviews its own portion and speaks up when something needs attention.

If you notice a problem, bring it up. Do not assume someone else noticed it.

8

Respect the person speaking

No side conversations and no whispering. One conversation at a time. If a topic only concerns two people, it belongs outside the meeting.

9

Be specific, not vague

Discuss actual problems. "The afternoon looks busy" is not useful. "At 2:00 we have two bondings and a consult requiring doctor time, we need to stagger the seating" is.

10

Bring solutions, not only complaints

Do not simply identify problems. Come prepared to explain the problem clearly and to say what you think we should do about it.

11

Nothing leaves without an owner and a date

Every action item names one person, one due date, and one follow-up. A task assigned to the team is a task assigned to nobody.

Do not finish with "everyone knows now." Finish with "this is the new documented process, this person owns it, and this is when we will review it."

12

Move long discussions somewhere else

If one topic needs much longer than the agenda allows, name it, assign a smaller working group, and continue outside the meeting.

13

Keep private information private

Financial details, personal information, and anything not needed for the team to operate stays out of the room. Share only what the team needs to do its work.

14

Anyone who missed it gets brought up to speed

Team members who were not present are still responsible for knowing what was discussed. They review the minutes and check with their team leader. If a change affects how they do their job, the team leader reviews it with them and confirms understanding.

Do not allow a system change to fail because one person was not present.
